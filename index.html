About the Conference

Energy infrastructure is evolving into cyber-physical intelligence

Bridging electrical power engineering, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, edge computing, and sustainable e-mobility.

Rising electricity demand, global decarbonisation targets, the transition of conventional consumers to active prosumers, and the requirement of ultra-secure communication interfaces are transforming traditional, infrastructure-centric power grids into complex, bidirectional, data-rich cyber-physical energy systems (CPES). Physical assets—generation units, transmission and distribution networks, microgrids, battery storage, electric vehicles, and smart appliances—are now tightly coupled with sensing, communication, computing, and automated control layers.

While this digital convergence unlocks transformative opportunities for Artificial Intelligence (AI)—transforming telemetry from SCADA systems, smart meters, PMUs, IIoT sensors, and weather models into real-time forecasting, state estimation, and autonomous control—it simultaneously introduces cross-layer cyber-physical vulnerabilities, cascading fault risks, and climate-induced disturbances.

Organised jointly by Maharaja Agrasen Institute of Technology (MAIT), Delhi, India and Birmingham City University (BCU), United Kingdom under the IEEE Smart Cities initiative, AIR-CPES 2027 provides a premier interdisciplinary forum connecting power engineering, computer science, machine learning, communications, cybersecurity, and energy policy.

Springer LNEE · Volume I (~50 Papers, ~430 Pages)

AI for Smart Grids, Microgrids & Cyber-Physical Resilience

Explores AI across the planning and operation of smart grids, microgrids, and distributed energy systems—data-driven forecasting and optimisation, AI-based monitoring, control, protection, fault diagnosis, digital twins, predictive maintenance, and AI-enabled cyber-threat, intrusion, and anomaly detection for resilient, self-healing architectures.

Springer LNEE · Volume II (~50 Papers, ~430 Pages)

Energy Storage, Electric Mobility, Smart Cities & Decentralised Systems

Focuses on the synergy between energy networks, electric mobility, and storage—AI-enabled battery management, SoC/SoH/RUL estimation, second-life battery assessment, smart charging, battery swapping, V2G/V2B, intelligent P2P energy markets, smart buildings and cities, and rural/decentralised microgrids in emerging economies.

    Quality Assurance

    Double-Blind Peer Review & Publishing Policy

    • 1
      Strict Double-Blind Review: Author names, affiliations, email addresses, and self-identifying acknowledgements must be omitted from the submitted PDF manuscript. Non-compliant papers are desk-rejected without review.
    • 2
      Minimum 3 Independent Reviewers: Every manuscript is evaluated by at least three domain reviewers (maximum 10 papers assigned per reviewer). Acceptance requires positive recommendations from at least two reviewers and final approval by the Conference Chair.
    • 3
      Turnitin Plagiarism Screening (< 15%): All submissions are screened using Turnitin. The overall similarity index must be strictly below 15% (with < 2% from any single source). AI-generated/SciGen-fabricated manuscripts and dual submissions are summarily rejected.
    • 4
      Formatting & Page Limit: Maximum 8 pages including figures, tables, and references in Springer LNEE format, submitted in PDF without page numbers or custom headers/footers.
    • 5
      Mandatory Registration & Presentation: At least one author of each accepted paper must register and present the paper at the conference (in-person at MAIT/BCU or live hybrid) for inclusion in the Springer proceedings.
